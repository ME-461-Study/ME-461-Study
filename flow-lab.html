θ–δ–M explorer

Deflection δ against shock angle θ. Each curve is one upstream Mach number; it rises from the Mach angle, peaks at δmax and returns to zero at a normal shock. A horizontal line at your δ cuts the bold curve at the weak and strong solutions.

Solutions

Dashed: maximum-deflection locus. Dotted: M₂ = 1 locus. Between them lies the small subsonic part of the weak branch.

Moving source and the Mach cone

A point source emits a pulse every tick; each pulse grows at the sound speed from where it was emitted. Above M = 1 the source outruns its own pulses and the envelope forms the Mach cone. Switch to accelerating to see why an instantaneous cone fails (HW 2 #3b).

Motion

Readout

Inside the cone: zone of action. Outside: zone of silence for this source. The cone describes infinitesimal disturbances, not a finite shock.

Supersonic flow at a corner

Turn the wall into the flow for a compression corner (oblique shock) or away from it for an expansion fan. For compression you can switch to a smooth turn, where the Mach waves converge and the flow stays isentropic until they coalesce.

First cell of a 2D jet

The upper half of a symmetric jet leaving a nozzle lip. The free boundary holds p = p_amb; the centerline acts like a wall. Set the exit Mach number and the pressure mismatch to follow the first waves (the HW 4 #1 pattern for an over-expanded jet).
